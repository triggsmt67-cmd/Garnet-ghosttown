# Garnet research source

The original research packet is stored here as `garnet-montana-ghost-town-research.pdf`.
It is a 16-page source compilation covering geology, mining economics, community life,
architecture, decline, preservation, recreation, and a bibliography of 51 references.

## Visitor-facing themes worth reusing

- Garnet was built quickly and without a formal town plan; many timber buildings were
  placed directly on the ground, which helps explain both their character and fragility.
- The town grew into a family community rather than remaining only a transient mining
  camp. Its school served 41 students at the height of the boom, while dances, picnics,
  sewing circles, and community gatherings filled the calendar.
- The J.K. Wells Hotel, Davey's Store, Kelly's Saloon, the Adams House, and the Ritchey
  home reveal distinct layers of public, commercial, and domestic life.
- Newspaper and catalog pages used as later wall insulation became an accidental archive
  of everyday life.
- "Arrested decay" means stabilizing original buildings without making them look newly
  reconstructed. Modern footings, bracing, and roof work are deliberately kept discreet.
- Garnet's isolation is part of the experience, but practical visitor details should be
  confirmed against current BLM guidance and WordPress-managed road reports.

## Editorial caution

Use the packet as a research lead, not as the live source of truth for prices, road dates,
event dates, access conditions, contact details, or program availability. Those details
should continue to come from the current BLM source or the site's WordPress fields.
